\section{Actor-Critique Pipeline}
\label{sec:actor-critique-results}

This section presents comprehensive experimental results for our actor-critique pipeline (Architecture 2), powered by Google Gemini 2.5 Flash \cite{google_gemini_2024}. We conduct an ablation study evaluating the system both with and without the critique validation step across five languages on the development set, analyzing narrative and subnarrative classification effectiveness, cross-linguistic performance patterns, and the specific impact of the validation mechanism on classification accuracy.

\subsection{Experimental setup and evaluation framework}

Our evaluation was conducted on the multilingual development set comprising 178 documents across five languages: Bulgarian (BG, 35 files), English (EN, 41 files), Hindi (HI, 35 files), Portuguese (PT, 35 files), and Russian (RU, 32 files). We evaluate two configurations of our actor-critique pipeline:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Without Validation}: A single-stage classification agent provides direct predictions for both narratives and subnarratives without additional validation.
    \item \textbf{With Validation}: A two-stage process where an initial classification agent provides predictions, followed by a critique agent that validates and refines \emph{only the narrative-level predictions}. Subnarrative classifications remain unchanged from the initial agent's predictions.
\end{itemize}

This ablation study aims to quantify the effectiveness of narrative-level critique validation in addressing error propagation and semantic ambiguity limitations at the first hierarchical level, while evaluating its indirect impact on subnarrative performance and computational overhead trade-offs.

Our primary evaluation metrics are F1 Coarse (for narratives) and F1 Sample (for subnarratives), providing balanced assessment of multi-label performance at both hierarchical levels by evaluating each document's label set independently.

\subsection{Key findings from ablation study}

\paragraph{Validation effectiveness is language-dependent.} The impact of the validation step varies significantly across languages, with no universal benefit pattern. Russian consistently benefits from validation across both hierarchical levels (+0.0122 F1 Coarse, +0.0032 F1 Sample), while Portuguese performs better without validation (-0.0154 F1 Coarse, -0.0056 F1 Sample). This suggests that validation effectiveness depends on language-specific linguistic characteristics and cultural context patterns.

\paragraph{Narrative-level validation affects subnarrative performance indirectly.} Since validation is applied only to narrative-level predictions, changes in subnarrative performance represent indirect effects of improved or degraded narrative classification accuracy. English demonstrates this clearly: direct narrative validation improvement (+0.0109) coincides with subnarrative performance degradation (-0.0140), suggesting that refined narrative predictions may create misalignment in the hierarchical classification process. Conversely, Hindi shows improved subnarrative classification (+0.0106) despite worse narrative performance (-0.0498), indicating that some validation-induced narrative changes may inadvertently benefit downstream subnarrative accuracy.

\paragraph{Performance ranking consistency despite validation variability.} Despite the mixed validation effects, language performance rankings remain consistent across both configurations. Portuguese maintains the highest overall performance (F1 Coarse: 0.7489 without validation, 0.7335 with validation), while Hindi consistently shows the lowest performance (F1 Coarse: 0.4715 without validation, 0.4217 with validation). This indicates that fundamental language processing capabilities dominate over architectural modifications.

\paragraph{Marginal overall impact with computational overhead.} The average performance difference across languages is minimal (-0.0100 F1 Coarse, -0.0022 F1 Sample), suggesting that narrative-level validation introduces computational overhead without systematic performance gains at either hierarchical level. The largest individual improvement (Hindi subnarratives: +0.0106) and degradation (Hindi narratives: -0.0498) indicate that narrative validation effects are highly context-specific, with indirect subnarrative impacts that may not be predictable from direct narrative changes.

\subsection{Hierarchical performance characteristics}

The actor-critique pipeline demonstrates a consistent pattern of performance degradation from narrative to subnarrative classification across all languages:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Average performance gap}: 19.4\% reduction from F1 Coarse (narratives, 0.6019) to F1 Sample (subnarratives, 0.4077).
    \item \textbf{Smallest gap}: English (13.2\% reduction).
    \item \textbf{Largest gap}: Portuguese (27.4\% reduction).
\end{itemize}

This hierarchical performance pattern is significantly smaller than observed in the transformer baseline (60\% reduction), suggesting that the actor-critique validation process effectively mitigates some error propagation issues inherent in hierarchical classification.

\subsection{Comparative analysis with baseline architectures}

Both actor-critique configurations demonstrate substantial improvements over the transformer baseline, with the choice between validation configurations depending on specific deployment requirements:

\paragraph{Hierarchical error mitigation varies by configuration.} The without-validation configuration shows a 19.4\% average performance gap between narratives and subnarratives (F1 Coarse: 0.6019 vs F1 Sample: 0.4077), while the with-validation configuration shows an 18.6\% gap (F1 Coarse: 0.5919 vs F1 Sample: 0.4055). Both represent significant improvements over the transformer baseline's 60\% gap. Notably, the marginal improvement in hierarchical consistency with validation suggests that narrative-level refinement has limited direct impact on reducing the inherent difficulty of fine-grained subnarrative classification.

\paragraph{Peak performance without systematic validation benefit.} The without-validation configuration achieves the highest individual performance scores (Portuguese F1 Coarse: 0.7489), substantially exceeding the transformer baseline's best language performance. However, the validation configuration shows more conservative but potentially more reliable performance across languages.

\paragraph{Precision-recall trade-offs differ by configuration.} The validation mechanism affects precision-recall balance differently across languages, suggesting that optimal configuration choice should consider target application requirements. For applications prioritizing maximum precision, validation may be beneficial for specific languages (Russian), while applications requiring consistent recall may benefit from the simpler without-validation approach.

\subsection{Key insights and architectural implications}

\paragraph{Configuration-dependent validation effectiveness.} Our ablation study reveals that narrative-level validation effectiveness is highly dependent on language characteristics, with indirect and unpredictable effects on subnarrative performance. The validation step should be considered as a language-specific optimization for narrative classification rather than a universal architectural enhancement affecting both hierarchical levels.

\paragraph{Cost-benefit analysis favors simpler architecture.} The minimal average performance gains from validation (-0.0100 F1 Coarse, -0.0022 F1 Sample) combined with the computational overhead of the two-stage architecture suggest that the without-validation configuration may be optimal for most deployment scenarios, particularly when processing efficiency is prioritized.

\paragraph{Language-specific deployment strategies.} The differential narrative validation effects across languages (beneficial for Russian, detrimental for Portuguese) suggest that optimal deployment strategies should consider language-specific pipeline configurations for narrative-level processing. This finding has significant implications for multilingual production systems where different languages may benefit from different narrative validation approaches, while subnarrative processing remains consistent across configurations.

\paragraph{Hierarchical classification robustness.} Both configurations demonstrate substantial improvements over traditional baselines in hierarchical classification tasks, with the without-validation approach achieving the strongest individual performance metrics while the validation approach provides marginally more consistent cross-linguistic performance.

The actor-critique pipeline establishes a flexible foundation for zero-shot multilingual narrative classification, demonstrating that narrative-level architectural enhancements should be carefully balanced against performance gains and their indirect effects on hierarchical classification. The ablation study provides clear guidance for selecting optimal narrative validation configurations based on specific deployment requirements, language priorities, and computational constraints, while highlighting the complex interdependencies between hierarchical classification levels.
